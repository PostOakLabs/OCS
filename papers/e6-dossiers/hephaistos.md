# Case Dossier: Project Hephaistos (Dyson-sphere IR-excess candidates)

Compiled 2026-07-30. Raw sourced material only — no scoring, no framework application.

## Observational data as published

**Suazo et al. 2024 (Project Hephaistos II).** Matías Suazo, Erik Zackrisson, Priyatam K. Mahto, Fabian Lundell, Carl Nettelblad, Andreas J. Korn, Jason T. Wright, Suman Majumdar. "Project Hephaistos – II. Dyson sphere candidates from Gaia DR3, 2MASS, and WISE." Submitted arXiv 2024-05-05 (arXiv:2405.02927); published *MNRAS* 531(1), 695–707 (2024-05-06; corrected/typeset 2024-05-16), DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stae1186.
Method: pipeline searching ~5×10⁶ objects within 300 pc, cross-matching Gaia DR3 optical astrometry/photometry with 2MASS near-IR and WISE mid-IR photometry, using a CNN to flag WISE source confusion, to find stars with mid-IR excess (W3/W4 bands) consistent with re-radiated waste heat from a partial Dyson sphere. Pipeline output: 7 final candidates (labeled A–G in follow-up literature), all M dwarfs. Abstract statement: "astrophysical phenomena cannot easily account for the observed infrared excess emission" in these 7 objects.
GAP: could not verify from the abstract/summary alone whether the Suazo et al. 2024 full text itself flagged background-galaxy/line-of-sight contamination as a candidate explanation already considered and left open, versus this being raised only by later authors — WebFetch on the arXiv abstract page returned only the abstract, not full-text discussion/caveat sections. Full PDF was not read in this pass.

**Ren, Garrett & Siemion 2024.** Tongtian Ren, Michael A. Garrett, Andrew P. V. Siemion. "Background Contamination of the Project Hephaistos Dyson Spheres Candidates." arXiv:2405.14921, submitted 2024-05-23.
Method: cross-matched 3 of the 7 candidates (A, B, G) against radio all-sky survey catalogues. Found radio counterparts offset from the Gaia stellar positions by ~4.9″ (A), ~0.4″ (B), ~5.0″ (G). Estimated the areal sky density of Hot Dust-Obscured Galaxies (Hot DOGs) at ~9×10⁻⁶ arcsec⁻². Conclusion: radio-detected dust-obscured background galaxies along the line of sight elevate WISE W3/W4 flux for these M dwarfs, and Hot DOG surface density is high enough to plausibly account for all 7 candidates as chance alignments.

**Blain 2024.** Andrew W. Blain. "Did WISE detect Dyson Spheres/Structures around Gaia-2MASS-selected stars?" arXiv:2409.11447, submitted 2024-09-17. Marked "Submitted to MNRAS, possibly letters" on arXiv — GAP: could not confirm final journal publication venue/status; only the preprint was verified.
Content (verbatim abstract excerpt): "...a trawl of several million potentially-habitable Gaia-detected stars for mid-IR-excess signatures is fraught with danger, due to both noise from such a large sample and, more importantly, confusion with the emission from dusty background galaxies. In light of a recent claim of seven potential DSMs in MNRAS, a brief rebuttal appeared on arXiv [i.e., Ren et al. 2024]. Further to this response, the relevance of WISE-detected galaxies is discussed in more detail, leading to a seemingly tight limit on the number and lifetime of DSMs... in the ~600-pc-radius region patrolled by Gaia. However, the detectability of DSMs is questioned: a DSM might extinguish its star at optical/near-IR wavelengths... Moreover, a civilization advanced enough to construct a DSM is likely to be advanced enough to use countermeasures to mask its presence."
This paper both (a) extends the background-galaxy-contamination argument statistically and (b) explicitly raises a non-detection/selection-effect caveat: absence of a resolved excess is not strong evidence against Dyson spheres in general, only against this specific unmasked, catalog-visible variety.

**Ren, Garrett & Siemion 2025.** Tongtian Ren, Michael A. Garrett, Andrew P. V. Siemion, plus ~22 co-authors. "High-resolution imaging of the radio source associated with Project Hephaistos Dyson Sphere Candidate G." arXiv:2501.05152, submitted 2025-01-09; published *MNRAS* 538, L56–L61 (2025).
Method: e-MERLIN (1.5 GHz) and EVN (5 GHz, e-VLBI) high-resolution radio imaging of the radio source associated with candidate G. Result: source resolves into 3 compact components with brightness temperature >10⁸ K, characteristic of a radio-loud AGN; no radio emission detected at the M-dwarf's own position. Conclusion: candidate G's mid-IR excess originates in a background AGN, not the star.

**Ren, Garrett, Zackrisson, Korn, Siemion, Wright, Brandeker et al. 2026.** "Archival Diagnostics for Potential Background Contaminants of Project Hephaistos Dyson Sphere Candidates." arXiv:2607.03619, submitted 2026-07-03 (~43 authors total).
Method: archival-only diagnostics (no new observing time) — Gaia astrometric positions propagated to the AllWISE epoch and compared against AllWISE mid-IR centroids, deep archival optical/near-IR imaging, and radio spectral-index measurements, applied across 9 candidates (the original 7 plus 2 of the 3 added by Hephaistos III, see below).
Findings by candidate: B and C show significant contamination signatures (B: radio counterpart, spectral index 0.63±0.11; C: near-IR companion offset 3.75″). A, E, F, H, J show weaker/marginal contamination indicators. D and I show no obvious contamination signal in archival data and are flagged as requiring dedicated follow-up observations.

**Korn, Suazo, Zackrisson, Cortés-Zuleta, Rains, Nabizadeh 2026.** "Project Hephaistos – III. Characterizing anomalous infrared sources identified as Dyson-sphere candidates." arXiv:2607.25701, submitted 2026-07-28.
Method: new photometric and spectroscopic follow-up of the original 7 candidates plus 3 additional similar sources found since 2024 (10 objects total). Findings: stellar parameters show no irregularities; IR properties resemble transitional circumstellar disks but spectra lack youth signatures expected of young stellar objects; one target shows weak Hα attributed to stellar chromospheric activity, not accretion. Two of the ten targets show superposition with very red background galaxies in JWST imaging (these are candidates D and E, resolved in the companion paper below). Stated conclusion: "we still find no clear explanation for the infrared excess of these stars" for the remainder, while noting JWST/ALMA could discriminate dust emission from background sources.

**Zackrisson, Bik, Ali Asgar, Curtis, Wright, Ren, Assef, Blain, Brandeker, Garrett, Gorti, Korn, Mahto, Nabizadeh 2026.** "Project Hephaistos – IV. James Webb Space Telescope Observations of Two Dyson Sphere Candidates." arXiv:2607.09460, submitted 2026-07-10.
Method: JWST/MIRI imaging and spectroscopy of candidates D and E. Findings: Candidate D — point-source-dominated imaging at z≈0.9, spectrum consistent with a Hot Dust-Obscured Galaxy. Candidate E — extended morphology with bright knots at z≈0.4, spectrum consistent with a dusty starburst galaxy. Both sources are projected within ~1″ of the M dwarfs. Stated conclusion: "the infrared excess does not originate from Dysonian megastructures... but from background galaxies projected within ~1 arcsec of the M dwarfs" (candidates D and E specifically).

## Hypothesis menu — epoch by epoch

### Epoch 1: 2024-05-05/06, Suazo et al. 2024 (Project Hephaistos II) publication

At publication, the paper reports a pipeline-selected sample of 7 M dwarfs with WISE W3/W4 mid-IR excess surviving a CNN-based rejection filter meant to remove obvious WISE source confusion. The abstract frames the excess as not easily explained by "astrophysical phenomena" and positions the 7 objects within the Dyson-sphere/technosignature search framework. On the face of the abstract, the hypothesis menu at this point is: (i) genuine circumstellar waste-heat structure (Dyson sphere/technosignature candidate, per the paper's stated framing), (ii) an as-yet-unidentified ordinary astrophysical source of IR excess around an M dwarf (debris disk, etc., which the abstract says "cannot easily account for" the signal but does not fully exclude), or (iii) a data/pipeline artifact not caught by the CNN filter. Whether the authors' own full text already flagged line-of-sight background-galaxy contamination as a live, unresolved alternative at this stage is a documented gap below (full text not read); the CNN step was explicitly built to filter WISE "confusion," which is adjacent to but not necessarily identical to the specific background-DOG-contamination hypothesis raised by the next round of papers.

### Epoch 2: 2024-05-23 to 2024-09-17, first discriminating follow-up (Ren et al. 2024, Blain 2024)

Within roughly three weeks of publication, Ren, Garrett & Siemion (2024) cross-matched 3 of the 7 candidates (A, B, G) against radio catalogues and found off-star radio counterparts, introducing "background dust-obscured galaxy chance alignment" as a specific, testable rival hypothesis, backed by an estimated Hot DOG areal density. Blain (2024), responding to both the original claim and the Ren et al. rebuttal, extended this into a statistical argument that Hot DOG surface density alone is high enough to plausibly account for all 7 candidates by chance superposition, while also introducing a distinct methodological caveat: that WISE-based searches are structurally poor at ruling out Dyson spheres in general, since an advanced structure could suppress its star's optical/near-IR flux (evading the Gaia/2MASS legs of the original pipeline) or otherwise be deliberately undetectable. At this epoch the menu is: (i) Dyson sphere/technosignature (still open for candidates other than A, B, G, and even for those three not strictly excluded without direct imaging), (ii) background dust-obscured-galaxy chance alignment (now the leading rival, supported by radio positional offsets and Hot DOG statistics), (iii) other unidentified astrophysical excess.

### Epoch 3: 2025-01-09, targeted high-resolution radio follow-up (Ren, Garrett & Siemion 2025)

e-MERLIN/EVN VLBI imaging of the radio source associated specifically with candidate G resolves it into 3 compact, high-brightness-temperature (>10⁸ K) components at the radio position (not the stellar position), diagnostic of a radio-loud AGN. This is the first candidate-by-candidate direct resolution: candidate G is reclassified from "Dyson-sphere candidate" to "background AGN contaminant," closing the technosignature hypothesis for that specific object on structural/imaging grounds rather than statistical argument alone. The menu for the remaining 6 candidates (A, B, C, D, E, F) is unchanged from Epoch 2.

### Epoch 4: 2026-07-03, archival diagnostics across an expanded candidate set (Ren et al. 2026)

By this point the candidate list has grown to include 3 additional similar sources found independently (see Hephaistos III, Epoch 5), and this paper applies archival-only diagnostics (Gaia/AllWISE astrometric offsets, deep archival imaging, radio spectral indices) across 9 of the (by now) 10 candidates. B and C are flagged with strong contamination evidence (radio counterpart for B; offset near-IR companion for C); A, E, F, H, J show weaker/marginal evidence; D and I show no contamination signature in archival data and are explicitly called out as needing new dedicated observations. At this epoch the menu bifurcates by object: several candidates (B, C, and with lower confidence A, E, F, H, J) are trending toward the background-contamination explanation, while D and I remain fully open, including for the Dyson-sphere/technosignature hypothesis, precisely because archival data could not adjudicate them.

### Epoch 5: 2026-07-10 and 2026-07-28, JWST resolution and full follow-up summary (Zackrisson et al. 2026; Korn et al. 2026)

JWST/MIRI imaging and spectroscopy (submitted 2026-07-10) directly resolves candidates D and E, the two objects the archival-diagnostics paper one week earlier had flagged as unresolved, into background galaxies projected within ~1″ of the target M dwarfs: D is a z≈0.9 Hot Dust-Obscured Galaxy, E is a z≈0.4 dusty starburst galaxy. Project Hephaistos III (submitted 2026-07-28, the most recent paper in this dossier), reporting spectroscopic/photometric follow-up on all 10 candidates (original 7 plus 3 added since), states that stellar parameters for the M dwarfs themselves are unremarkable, that the IR excess shapes resemble transitional circumstellar disks but lack the spectroscopic youth signatures expected of genuine young stellar objects, and that two of the ten targets (D and E, per the companion JWST paper) are now confirmed background-galaxy superpositions. For the remaining candidates the paper states explicitly: "we still find no clear explanation for the infrared excess of these stars," while noting JWST/ALMA as the discriminating tools going forward.

## Resolution

As of the most recent paper read in this dossier (Korn et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.25701, submitted 2026-07-28), Project Hephaistos is a **partial, ongoing resolution, not a closed case**:
- Candidate G: resolved as a background radio-loud AGN via direct VLBI imaging (Ren, Garrett & Siemion 2025).
- Candidates D and E: resolved as background galaxies (a Hot DOG and a dusty starburst, respectively) via direct JWST imaging/spectroscopy (Zackrisson et al. 2026).
- Candidates B and C: strong archival evidence (radio counterpart; offset near-IR companion) for background contamination, but not confirmed by direct high-resolution imaging in the sources reviewed here.
- Candidates A, E [note: E already resolved above], F, H, J: weaker/marginal archival contamination evidence per Ren et al. 2026; not confirmed.
- Candidates D [already resolved], I: no contamination signature found in archival data as of Ren et al. 2026; status as of the most recent paper (Korn et al. 2026) is "no clear explanation," i.e., still formally open, with the Dyson-sphere/technosignature hypothesis not excluded for these specific objects by any paper reviewed here.
GAP: could not verify a single terminal "the anomaly is resolved" statement from any primary source in this set — every paper explicitly leaves at least some of the 10 total candidates (original 7 + 3 added) unresolved. The framework-narrative arc (unexplained excess → background-contamination hypothesis → statistical support → direct imaging confirmation for a subset) is well sourced, but "dust-obscured background galaxy" as the resolution for the full original 7 is confirmed directly for only 3 of them (G, D, E) as of 2026-07-28; the rest rests on statistical/archival argument (Blain 2024; Ren et al. 2024, 2026) rather than direct per-object confirmation.
