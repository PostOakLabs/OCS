Anisotropy Degeneracy Explorer

The sharpest objection to kinematic IMBH evidence: radial velocity anisotropy (β > 0) and a concentrated remnant cluster can both mimic a central dispersion cusp. Adjust all three and see which combinations reproduce the observed ωCen profile — and when an IMBH becomes genuinely detectable.

Physics & citations

Uses the spherical isotropic Jeans equation with constant anisotropy parameter β (Binney & Tremaine §4.8.2). The tracer density follows a Plummer model (Mcl = 4 × 10⁶ M☉, a = 7 pc). The remnant cluster is an independent Plummer component contributing dynamical mass but no luminous tracers. The IMBH is a point mass at the centre.

Jeans solution (constant β): ρσ_r²(r) = r^{−2β} ∫_r^∞ ρ(r′) G M_enc(r′)/r′² · r′^{2β} dr′

Projected LOS dispersion via Abel integral: σ_los²(R) = ∫₀^∞ (1 − βR²/r²) (ρ_cl/ρ_tot) ρ_tot σ_r² dz / ∫₀^∞ ρ_cl dz, where r = √(R² + z²).

Reference profile (grey) is approximate — derived from the published oMEGACat VI data summary (Häberle et al. 2025, proper motions + radial velocities). Uncertainties are illustrative.
